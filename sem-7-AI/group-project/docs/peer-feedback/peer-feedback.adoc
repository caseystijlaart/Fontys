== Purpose of Peer Feedback

Within the FitPhone group project, a peer feedback round was conducted at the end of the semester.
Each team member assessed all other members using a structured form covering four categories: contribution to the project, collaboration and communication, responsibility and reliability, and effort and initiative.
Each category was scored on a scale of 1 to 10 and accompanied by a qualitative section for strengths and improvement points.

The reason peer feedback is conducted is that it provides a perspective that neither self-reflection nor teacher assessment alone can offer.
Peers observe your day-to-day behavior in the group, how you communicate under pressure, whether you follow through on tasks, and how you contribute to the team dynamic.
This makes peer feedback especially valuable for identifying blind spots, patterns of behavior that you may not notice yourself but that others experience consistently.
It also reinforces accountability within the team, because knowing that colleagues will reflect on your performance encourages everyone to stay engaged and take their responsibilities seriously.
Beyond evaluation, peer feedback is a learning tool. It provides structured input that can be used to grow both as a professional and as a team member.

=== Strengths

Multiple team members highlighted the same strengths, which gives these points particular credibility:

* *Taking initiative and structure:* Laelie, Stijn, Jeffrey, and Trang all noted that I regularly prepared presentations for meetings, kept notes for the group, and took on organizational tasks that kept things moving.
Trang specifically noted: "He ensures structure by preparing presentations and keeping notes, and steps in when someone else cannot take over."
* *Active involvement and helping others:* Arthur, Thomas, and Jeffrey described me as actively engaged, quick to pick things up, and willing to help when others needed support.
Arthur noted: "Often present, ready to help, presents when others cannot, communicates at the moments when it matters."
* *Communication and social skills:* Stijn and Jeffrey highlighted that I am pleasant to work with, socially engaging, and contribute well during client conversations.

=== Improvement Points

The most striking aspect of the received feedback is how consistently a single improvement point was raised.
Four out of six reviewers independently pointed out the same thing: I do not ask for help quickly enough when I am stuck, and I tend to try to solve things on my own rather than being open about it.

* Laelie: "Can ask for help a bit more often and communicate when support is needed."
* Thomas: "He does not always ask for help quickly when something is not working out. As a result, he may spend longer searching for a solution than necessary."
* Stijn: "He could ask for help more often when needed."
* Trang: "Casey may more often indicate when he is struggling or needs help. He seems to solve a lot on his own, but more openness about this would make the collaboration smoother."

Additionally, Jeffrey noted that task ownership and division could be communicated more clearly within the project to keep everyone aligned.
Stijn and Arthur briefly mentioned attendance as a point of attention.

== Key Takeaways

Looking at the feedback as a whole, two main takeaways stand out:

=== Takeaway 1: Asking for Help is a Strength, Not a Weakness

The single most consistent piece of feedback I received was that I tend to work through problems independently without communicating that I am struggling.
Four different people noticed this from their own perspective, which means it is not a one-off observation but a genuine behavioral pattern.

This is something I can recognize in myself.
When I encounter a problem, my instinct is to keep working at it until I find the answer myself.
While this can be a useful quality in terms of persistence, it can also lead to spending more time on a problem than necessary, and it can leave the team unaware of where things stand.
In a group project, that lack of transparency can create bottlenecks without others even knowing there is something to resolve.

The feedback makes it clear that asking for help is not seen as a weakness by my team members. On the contrary, it would make the collaboration flow more smoothly for everyone.

=== Takeaway 2: Organizational Contributions Are Recognized and Valued

It was reassuring to see that the initiative I took in terms of preparing meetings, keeping notes, and ensuring structure was recognized and appreciated by multiple team members.
This is something I often do without thinking about it as a deliberate contribution, so seeing it reflected back as something the team found genuinely helpful is valuable.
It confirms that these kinds of behind-the-scenes organizational tasks have a real impact on group dynamics and project progress.

== How I Will Use This Feedback

The feedback gives me two clear and actionable directions for personal growth.

=== Communicating More Openly When Stuck

Going forward, I want to work on lowering my threshold for asking for help or flagging when something is taking longer than expected.
In practice, this means being more proactive about mentioning blockers during stand-ups or check-ins, rather than waiting until I have already spent considerable time on the issue.
It also means being more transparent about task progress in general, so the team has a clearer picture of where things stand.

This applies not only in group projects but also in professional contexts.
In a work environment, being able to signal when you need input or are facing uncertainty is an important skill, and it reflects well on someone's self-awareness and communication.

=== Building on Organizational Strengths

The positive feedback around structure and preparation reinforces that this is an area I should continue to invest in.
Taking ownership of meeting preparation, documentation, and keeping an overview of what needs to happen is something that adds value to a team, and it is something I feel comfortable doing.
In future projects, I want to formalize this a bit more, for example by explicitly taking on a coordination role at the start of a project rather than doing it informally.

=== Reflection on Giving Feedback

As part of this peer feedback round, I also provided feedback to all six of my team members.
This was a meaningful exercise in itself.
It required me to think carefully and honestly about how each person contributed, and to frame observations constructively rather than simply listing positives.
Writing feedback for others made me more aware of how I wanted to be evaluated myself, and reinforced the value of being specific and grounded rather than vague when sharing observations.

== Acting on the Feedback

After receiving this feedback, I made a conscious effort during the remainder of the semester to act on the points raised.
The clearest message from my teammates was that I needed to be more communicative and more openly involved with what was happening in the team, rather than keeping things to myself.

One of the first things I tried to change was being more intentional about checking in with teammates, not just regarding my own work but also theirs.
By paying more attention to what others were working on and where they might be running into difficulties, I got a better sense of where I could be useful and where collaboration made more sense than working in isolation.
This also made it easier to speak up about my own progress, since being more involved in the overall picture naturally lowered the threshold for raising a concern or asking a question.

These were not dramatic changes, but gradual shifts in how I engaged with the team day to day, and they reflected a genuine attempt to put the feedback into practice rather than simply noting it and moving on.

== Conclusion

The peer feedback round was a valuable moment of reflection, both in terms of what I learned about myself and in terms of understanding the team dynamic throughout the semester.
The consistent nature of the improvement points, especially around asking for help, gives me something concrete to work on.
The recognition of my strengths in initiative and structure gives me confidence that my contributions were visible and meaningful.

Overall, I take this feedback seriously and intend to carry the lessons from it forward, both in the remainder of this minor and in my future professional development.
