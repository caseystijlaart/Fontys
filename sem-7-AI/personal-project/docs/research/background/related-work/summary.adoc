= Summary of Existing Plant Monitoring Systems

Plant monitoring systems can be broadly categorized into three main groups:

* **IoT-Based Systems (Non-AI/ML):**  
These systems monitor environmental parameters such as soil moisture, temperature, and humidity, often sending alerts when thresholds are exceeded.
Some include basic automation, like watering when soil moisture is low.
They are widely implemented in open-source hobbyist projects and student prototypes.
However, they lack AI/ML capabilities for predictive or adaptive care.

* **AI/ML-Based Systems:**  
Recent research and prototype systems integrate AI/ML to predict irrigation needs, detect plant stress or disease, and provide intelligent recommendations.
These systems are still largely experimental or research-based, with limited adoption in consumer-grade products.
They demonstrate the potential for proactive, adaptive plant care that goes beyond threshold-based monitoring.

* **Commercial Solutions:**  
Commercial products primarily target large-scale agriculture, monitoring crop health across fields using sophisticated sensors and IoT platforms.
These solutions are highly capable but are not designed for regular indoor or household plants, and they are often cost-prohibitive for small-scale applications.

Despite these developments, there is a clear gap for affordable, small-scale AI/ML-based plant health monitoring systems suitable for hobbyists or home users.
Most current systems either:  

1. Focus on simple threshold-based alerts (IoT-only). 
2. Are experimental research prototypes (AI/ML). 
3. Are expensive and targeted at commercial agriculture. 

There is no widely-available, low-cost solution that combines AI/ML intelligence with IoT for personal plant care.
