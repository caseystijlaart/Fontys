= Conclusion

RQ6 (minimal data set, avoiding personally identifiable information) is satisfied by design.
mmWave point-cloud output, a general height estimate, and a speaker-attribution angle carry no imagery, biometric template, or persistent identifier (Legal Framework, Data-Handling Design).
The confirmed design goes further: tracked people are held only under a transient label such as "Person A", never a name, and all information about them is dropped the moment they leave the sensor's field of view.
The microphone array only identifies which tracked person is speaking. It never processes or stores what is said, and never performs biometric voice recognition.

RQ7 (applicable AVG/GDPR obligations and how to design for them) is answered as follows.
The system's output is very likely to qualify as anonymous rather than personal data under the GDPR's Recital 26/WP216 test, since no cross-session linkage exists, none is planned, and nothing survives a person leaving frame.
That conclusion rests on the data-handling design in Data-Handling Design actually being built as specified, not on the sensor choice alone.
Article 8 GDPR's child-consent mechanism does not apply to this physical installation.
Recital 38's general instruction to hold children's data to a higher necessity/proportionality standard does apply, and is met by aiming for anonymity rather than relying on a legitimate-interest balancing test.
The installation being known and avoidable, visitors choose to come and can stay out of the sensor's view, is a further mitigating factor, though it does not replace the transparency and DPIA-screening recommendations below.

Two items remain open. They are recommended as preconditions before the installation is used with real child visitors, not as blockers to the Phase 1-3 prototyping work already planned.

* Verify, once the pipeline and microphone array are built, that the implementation matches this chapter's confirmed design: frame-exit drop, transient labels only, and no recording, transcript, or voiceprint from the microphone array (Data-Handling Design, Prototyping Plan Phase 4).
* Add visitor/parent-facing signage near the installation (Legal Framework), and screen for whether a formal Data Protection Impact Assessment is required. Two of the Article 29 Working Party's DPIA trigger criteria plausibly apply here: vulnerable subjects (children) and systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area.

A standalone, visitor-facing privacy notice or policy document is a natural next deliverable building on this analysis. It is out of scope for this chapter.
