[[mmwave-module-selection]]
= Module Selection

Candidate Sensor Technologies recommends mmWave radar as the sensing modality, built around the TI IWR6843 chip.
The chip ships on several evaluation-board variants at different price points and field-of-view characteristics, given below as azimuth (angular coverage side-to-side) / elevation (angular coverage top-to-bottom, i.e. floor-to-head reach), all sharing the same TI mmWave SDK/point-cloud software stack and all operable standalone over USB (none strictly require the separate MMWAVEICBOOST carrier card for point-cloud demos).
This chapter compares those variants directly and picks one.
None of these professional evaluation boards are stocked by Dutch hobbyist-electronics shops (Kiwi Electronics, Antratek, TinyTronics); the actual sourcing path for a Dutch buyer is DigiKey's or Mouser's Netherlands storefronts, both of which ship locally and quote in EUR, which is why the links and prices below use those stores.

* link:https://www.ti.com/tool/IWR6843ISK[IWR6843ISK]: the standard long-range antenna plug-in module, €248,23 at current link:https://nl.mouser.com/en/ProductDetail/Texas-Instruments/IWR6843ISK?qs=PqoDHHvF64%252BpLvVHE%2FA5bw%3D%3D[Mouser NL] listings, with a 120° azimuth / a narrower 30° elevation field of view tuned for range over coverage.
Better documented in some community projects, but its narrow elevation beam works against a room-scale height-estimation use case rather than for it.
* link:https://www.ti.com/tool/IWR6843ISK-ODS[IWR6843ISK-ODS]: a wide-field-of-view (120° azimuth / 120° elevation) antenna variant, etched on a Rogers substrate, that TI markets specifically for overhead/ceiling-mounted people counting and tracking, ~12 m range. €205,44 at current link:https://nl.mouser.com/en/ProductDetail/Texas-Instruments/IWR6843ISK-ODS?qs=9r4v7xj2LnlHBzZTGNiR9A%3D%3D[Mouser].
Better suited than the plain ISK if the Holobox sensor ends up ceiling-mounted, since the use case is exactly what this variant's antenna is designed for.
* link:https://www.ti.com/tool/IWR6843AOPEVM[IWR6843AOPEVM]/link:https://www.ti.com/tool/AWR6843AOPEVM[AWR6843AOPEVM]: an antenna-on-package (AoP) variant, with the antenna integrated directly into the chip package rather than etched onto a separate PCB, €174,92 at current link:https://nl.mouser.com/en/ProductDetail/Texas-Instruments/IWR6843AOPEVM?qs=%252B6g0mu59x7L00bHiTWJzSw%3D%3D[Mouser NL]/link:https://nl.mouser.com/en/ProductDetail/Texas-Instruments/AWR6843AOPEVM?qs=QNEnbhJQKvYqpH36NBFH4w%3D%3D[AWR6483AOPEVM].
Matches the ISK-ODS's wide 120° azimuth / 120° elevation field of view in a smaller (~65×60mm), standard (non-overhead-specific) form factor, at the lowest price of the three variants compared.

[[mmwave-module-recommendation]]
== Recommendation

Of the three variants in <<mmwave-evm-comparison>>, the IWR6843AOPEVM (equivalently, its AWR6843AOPEVM sibling) is recommended.
Azimuth and elevation are not weighed equally, since they serve two different jobs.
Azimuth FOV and resolution govern how well the radar covers and separates people across the room's width.
This feeds RQ1's horizontal position tracking and the angular separation RQ4's multi-person detection depends on.
Elevation FOV and resolution instead govern how much of a person's vertical extent, floor to head, actually falls inside the radar's beam.
That is the precondition for the keypoint/pose-based height estimate described in Candidate Sensor Technologies: a head or shoulder joint outside the beam simply is not seen, regardless of range or azimuth coverage.
All three variants share the same 120° azimuth FOV, so azimuth cannot distinguish them at all.
Elevation is therefore the deciding criterion, not azimuth and not maximum range; every variant clears the range needed at this short-range, room-scale installation with margin to spare.

IWR6843ISK is ruled out directly on that basis.
Its elevation FOV is only 30°, by far the narrowest of the three, and its elevation angular resolution is also the coarsest at 58°, against 29° for the other two.
TI built this variant for long range over angular coverage, the opposite trade-off from what a short-range height reading needs, and it is also the most expensive variant at €248,23.
It loses on the criterion that matters and on price at the same time.
IWR6843ISK-ODS is the closest competitor, matching AOPEVM's 120° elevation FOV and 29° elevation angular resolution exactly.
Its maximum range differs from AOPEVM's by about a metre, which is not a meaningful difference for a short-range installation, so this is not treated as decisive either way.
What actually separates the two is price and mounting assumption.
ISK-ODS costs €205,44, around 17% more than AOPEVM, for no corresponding gain on the rows that matter here.
Its antenna is also etched specifically for overhead/ceiling-mounted installations, per TI's own positioning of the variant, and the Holobox's mounting position has not committed to that.
AOPEVM reaches the same elevation performance in a smaller (~65x60mm), mounting-agnostic form factor, at the lower of the two prices.

On every row that distinguishes the three variants, AOPEVM matches the best result or beats it outright, while also being the cheapest.
It is not a compromise between cost and capability the way ISK-ODS would be.
No other variant is preferable on any criterion that matters for this short-range use case.
This is why it is carried forward as the module to build with in the phased mmWave implementation set out in the Conclusion.
